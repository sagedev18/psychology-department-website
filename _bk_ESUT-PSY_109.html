8 ESUT-PSY 109 Suicide Markers and Prevention (2 Units; Core; LH= 30)

Senate-approved relevance

To produce graduates who are highly knowledgeable in the field of suicide markers and prevention in accordance with the vision and mission of Enugu State University of Science and Technology to utilize technology and learning outcomes for service to the society. Thus, Suicide Markers and Prevention will provide graduates of psychology with skills for the purpose of utilizing such in identifying causes of suicide, signs of suicidal ideation, reporting suspected suicidal ideation, preventing and managing suicide attempts in order to enhance physical, cognitive, emotional, psychological, social, economic and general well-being of individuals in families, communities, health, schools, educational, work and other related institutions in Enugu State and beyond.

Overview

Suicide is death caused by injuring oneself with the intent to die. It is the act of voluntarily causing oneâ€™s own death. Suicide poses major risks to public health worldwide. Suicidal behavior has been conceptualized as a continuum of thoughts and behavior ranging from suicidal ideation to completed suicide. Human suicide has been reported for more than 3000 years for social knowledge. In the ancient Greece, Egypt, Rome, Africa and even Igbo tribe, suicide was not allowed and regarded as sinful. Hence, the victimsâ€™ bodies would be abandoned in the forest and the family of the victimsâ€™ stigmatized. Suicide has been known in all cultures, every region and ethnic group. The first recorded suicides were committed by Pyramus and Thisbe, who were lovers that died in Babylonia, Persia around 2000 BC. Thus, Emile Durkheim (1897/1951) is being regarded as the founder of suicide owing to the wealth of his sociological study of suicide. This also results to Durkheim suicide theory being regarded as the most famous theory of suicide. The theory attributes suicide to social influences. Nevertheless, amidst numerous theories of suicides some such as interpersonal theory of suicide by (Joiner, 2005) seems to be ideal in addressing suicide because it seems to address all the phenomena associated with suicidal behavior.

This course is apt considering the socio-economic challenges faced by Nigerians especially residents of Enugu State which could trigger suicidal ideation and outright suicide. Recently, suicide ideation resulting in suicide seems to be on the increase among University undergraduates in Nigeria, hence the need for this course. The course objectives, learning outcomes and contents will equip graduates of psychology with the appropriate skills to help in the prevention of suicide and management of suicide attempts in different walks of in order to engender healthy society.

Objectives

The objectives of this course are to:

  • explain the meaning and menace of suicide;
  • narrate the historical antecedents of suicides;
  • explain the theories of suicides;
  • explain the aims of studying of suicide studies;
  • describe the causes and symptoms of suicide;
  • know the various preventive measures in suicide;
  • know the biological, psychological and social risk factors in suicide; and
  • identify the gate keepers in suicides and their roles in suicide prevention.

Learning Outcomes

On the completion of the course, students should be able to:

  • explain the meaning of suicide;
  • narrate the historical antecedents of suicides;
  • enumerate at least three theories of suicide;
  • describe at least five causes and symptoms of suicide;
  • identify at least five preventive measures in suicide;
  • explain at least three treatments of suicide;
  • enumerate at least three kinds of suicidal behavior;
  • differentiate among the biological, psychological and social risk factors in suicide; and
  • identify at least three gate keepers in suicide and the important roles they have to play to prevent suicides.

Course Contents

Scope of suicide. Overview of suicide markers. Causes and symptoms of suicide. Prevalence of suicide. Suicide attempts and completed suicide. Preparatory acts toward imminent suicidal behavior. Suicidal ideation. No deliberate self-harm. Self-harm behavior with unknown suicidal intent. Biological risk factors in suicide. Psychological risk factors in suicide. Proximal stressors in suicide. Primary prevention. Secondary prevention. Tertiary prevention. The important roles of gate keeper in suicide prevention. Psychological assessment and research methods in psychology.
